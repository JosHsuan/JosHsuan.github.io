## Step 5 proposal: restrained highlights and localized effects

The owner accepted cool gray materials against a charcoal background with a small amount of warm-white reflected light, along with the following material roles and localized effect treatment. Preserve the tattoo-derived dark masses, etched marks, and fine curves. Exact optical values, light placement, and particle levels remain to be tuned.

| Element | Accepted material/effect direction | Visual role and restraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notched slab | Smoked glass with visible thickness, etched internal marks, and soft edge reflections. | Retain the dark ink-like mass and recognizable notch while revealing depth during rotation. |
| Loop | Clearer glass with readable curved edges and a few concentrated reflections. | Supply the strongest glass cue without becoming a continuously glowing ring. |
| Calligraphic spine | Dark silver reflective metal with narrow highlights. | Let turning reveal its tapered profile; retain a dark body rather than filling the screen with bright reflections. |
| Fine curves | Thin off-white moving traces with tapered, fading trails. | Suggest recent movement. Trails shorten and fade as convergence slows. |
| Selected slab/spine regions | Small smoky gray particle clusters inspired by The Spirit. | Concentrate the effect at the slab's concave edge, with a smaller accent at the spine's hooked end. Keep the solid forms legible. |
| Airborne dust | Sparse, fine, softly lit grains across depth, with occasional defocused near particles. | Convey scale and atmosphere during transitions. Visibility comes from limited lit regions rather than uniform bright points throughout the view. |

Propose a broad soft light that reveals glass thickness and a restrained edge light that catches the metal. Warm-white accents appear in selected reflections and a few dust grains; most of the scene stays charcoal and cool gray. Prevent bright moving detail from competing with terminal text, and retain only soft, low-contrast background light through the frosted surface.

The accepted effect envelope now follows scroll progress. These interval boundaries are revised proposals:

- **0–15%:** Sparse visible dust, subtle reflections, and short traces support the open first view.
- **15–65%:** Rotation reveals the strongest material highlights. Selected trails and local smoke become more apparent as the objects converge; different objects peak at different progress values.
- **65–85%:** Trails shorten and local smoke diminishes as the forms settle. Avoid an abrupt flash or burst at assembly completion.
- **85–100%:** Keep the main forms settled and effects subdued while the terminal expands in the proposed handoff.
- **Reading state:** Keep a calm material silhouette and soft light. Let the remaining smoke and trails fade, retaining only occasional, very subtle dust motion, subject to step 6 motion preferences and graphics adaptations. Dust never gates terminal expansion.

The owner accepted the recommended cool gray treatment with a small amount of warm-white reflected light and effects that subside as the opening settles. This establishes the art direction, not exact brightness, particle counts, or shader parameters.

## Step 6: a scroll-controlled opening with a clear browsing handoff

The owner confirmed scrolling as the opening timeline rather than ordinary timed playback, then accepted the recommended reverse behavior and handoff. This replaces the prior proposal for six seconds of playback followed by a 0.8-second expansion. Do not wait for wheel scrolling to stop before advancing: it is now the intended control input. Terminal availability, frost, readable reflow, and the expanded end state remain established. The broad rules below are accepted; exact progress percentages, layout measurements, input handling, and the adaptation proposals remain to be refined.

### Working progress map

| Opening progress | Scene and interface |
| --- | --- |
| 0–15% | Sparse initial space; scrolling reveals independent drift and tumbling around the small usable terminal. |
| 15–65% | Camera travels forward/rightward, objects converge at their own rates, and selected highlights and effects grow more apparent. The camera settles before the forms. |

The percentages express authored sequence progress, not time or a fixed number of wheel notches. Total scroll distance remains open and should be tuned for mouse wheels, trackpads, and touch input in a later study.

### Input, reversal, and expansion

- Propose holding the scene in view during the short opening scroll range. Derive progress from scroll position rather than triggering a timed clip for each wheel event.
- When scrolling stops, main motion holds at that progress after any brief smoothing settles. Very subtle ambient dust may continue independently if motion is enabled; it cannot advance assembly or expansion.
- Allow upward scrolling to scrub backward within the unfinished opening, as accepted by the owner. Camera/object poses and the principal effect envelope should be reconstructible at the same progress without accumulating a different scene on each pass. Specific particle techniques remain an implementation decision.
- Map terminal expansion to the final progress interval rather than an elapsed-time animation. Keep comfortable text size, preserve the current page, reading anchor, focus, and any input or selection, and protect pressed targets from displacement. Propose roughly 4–6% viewport margins on desktop, subject to later layout review.
- Complete the handoff at the end of the opening and retain the expanded state during subsequent ordinary browsing, as accepted by the owner. Upward content scrolling does not shrink the terminal or reverse the camera. Any deliberate replay control or fresh-visit policy remains a separate, unconfirmed detail.

### Keep the terminal usable from the first view

- Scrolling the opening background advances the scene, while scrolling a scrollable area inside the terminal prioritizes reading its content. Preserve the accepted separation of responsibilities. The recommended boundary handling avoids driving both operations from one terminal gesture; exact behavior needs interaction testing.
- Provide a clear opening cue such as `Scroll to explore` and a direct `View work` action. The latter resolves the opening to its expanded reading state without requiring completion of the scenic scroll range, preserving any content already selected.
- Resolve how pointer, touch, and keyboard input select their scroll region during a later interface study. Do not impose a disabled terminal or block project navigation to force completion of the introduction.

### Adaptation proposals for later refinement

These remain recommendations for the eventual interface and implementation review, not additional owner-confirmed choices inferred from approval of reverse scrolling.

- Propose a nearly full-width terminal with a shorter initial height and an identifiable opening scroll area. A touch gesture in that opening area advances the same timeline; a gesture in terminal content reads that content. Expand primarily in height near the timeline's end.
- Recompose the background for narrow screens and reduce peripheral effects as needed. Exact dimensions and breakpoints remain open.
- Retain a `Motion` control for residual ambience. With reduced motion requested, propose the expanded terminal and a still final composition immediately, without a mandatory scenic scroll range.
- If 3D initialization fails or usable performance cannot be sustained, propose the same expanded interface with a static background. Interface loading and operation remain independent of graphics success; use a sufficiently opaque surface if blur is unavailable.
