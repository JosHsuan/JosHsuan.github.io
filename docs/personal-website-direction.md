## 1. Core Direction

Create **a continuous single-page experience built from the 3D assets of existing projects**, allowing visitors to experience the relationships between form, material, interaction, and making while clearly understanding the author's capabilities as a **Design Technologies Engineer**.

The five selected projects provide candidate forms and methods, including Caschlatsch, which the user has confirmed may be shared. Organize chapters around shared methods, with project sources and individual roles readable on the same page. The first version does not include separate project pages. Select individual files according to their provenance, attribution, and permitted public use.

Suggested central narrative: **How form is understood, transformed, and made, and how it becomes an actionable design method.**

Use a dark hero and large typography, with 3D progressively revealed through scrolling. Present method explanations and the professional summary in light reading sections. General visitors can experience the work first; employers and collaborators can go directly to capabilities, roles, and contact details.

## 5. Single-Page Experience Structure

The following is a proposed first-version narrative, not a set of completed scenes. Organize chapters by method and the order in which visitors need to understand them.

| Section | What visitors should understand or feel | Visuals and interaction | Readable content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and introduction | The author's professional direction and the presence of form and material | Dark background, name and positioning, a selected group of 3D assets | Name, professional title, positioning statement, language switch, and contact entry point |
| Form and transformation | How interaction produces different states of a form | Scrolling changes the viewpoint, relationships between elements, or display state; suitable excerpts support drag inspection | A short method explanation and source label |
| Making and process | How a method becomes real fabrication steps | Models and videos with step switching; transition into a light reading area | Step names, process details, and corresponding photographs or diagrams |
| Tools and interaction | How the author makes design methods usable and understandable | Publicly shareable interaction/XR material with concise explanations | Problem, method, individual role, and technical contribution |
| Professional summary and contact | The kinds of work to which the author can contribute | A light, directly readable information area | Brief introduction, capability summary, and contact details |

### Navigation

Use same-page anchors: `Experience / Approach / About / Contact`, with corresponding Traditional Chinese labels.

Visitors can follow the scrolling experience or jump directly to methods, capabilities, and contact details. Language switching preserves the current chapter and interaction state.

### Asset Explanations

Open a short information panel on the same page containing:

- Source project and year.
- The author's role, collaboration credits, and outcomes.
- The method or process currently shown.
- Corresponding images, videos, or diagrams.

Closing the panel returns to the original viewing position. These panels explain the assets; they do not introduce separate project routes.

### Verify Assets, Roles, and Permissions Separately

Existing preparation already records asset identifiers, provenance, and responsibilities. Reuse and verify those records first. The public inventory should contain only information suitable for publication.

- **Roles:** distinguish personal design/code, collaborative outcomes, fabrication assistance, photography and model authorship, course tools, and references.
- **Permissions:** retain the user's authorization for personally created assets free of company, client, or confidentiality restrictions, and the latest confirmation for Caschlatsch. Check other authors' images/models and still-restricted material individually.
- **Files:** candidates and previews in the preparation records remain private working material. A file's existence, inclusion in a portfolio, or successful conversion does not itself authorize publication.
- **Facts:** years, measurements, individual implementation scope, and outcome claims require corresponding evidence. Do not present unconfirmed information as an established result.

### Combining Elements Across Projects

Elements from different projects can establish visual relationships, compositions, and transitions. Explain real fabrication processes using their own records.

Label abstract deformation or transitions between projects as "visual interpretation" so visitors do not mistake them for a single shared process that actually occurred. Distinguish display effects and form animation from research records and validated engineering simulation.

## 7. Interaction Design

### Home Page: Scroll-Driven Narrative

Map page-scroll progress to camera, object pose, composition, and chapter state. Preserve natural scrolling so reading and scene state remain coordinated.

Begin with a small set of clear transition states and test the pacing. Identity and contact information must be directly readable; 3D loading must not block text.

### Form Inspection

Use this for assets whose surfaces, element relationships, scale, or details benefit from inspection. Provide a clear entry point, bounded rotation/zoom, and a view reset.

Support touch on mobile. Handle dragging in an explicit inspection mode while preserving page scrolling during ordinary reading.

### Process Steps

Use this where process records exist. Provide previous/next controls or directly selectable steps that synchronize model states, photographs/videos, and short explanations.

Build the steps from actual records. Real-time parametric simulation is not required in this version.

### Separate Content from the Scene

Use semantic HTML for identity, methods, sources, roles, and contact information. Canvas handles form, material, camera, and interaction.

A shared content structure connects sources, chapters, model states, and bilingual text. Every public asset retains a stable identifier, attribution, and provenance; scenes select and combine assets according to the narrative. Private source paths, evidence notes, and confidential information stay in private preparation storage.

Reuse conversion records for web copies and verify geometry, units, orientation, materials, and animation against the intended display. Clearly distinguish display models from actual fabrication files.

### Mobile and Fallbacks

The normal mobile experience directly presents simplified 3D in the same chapter order as desktop. Adjust model, material, texture, and postprocessing costs based on prototype results.

Show images and text first, load inexpensive hero assets early, and load later chapters as needed. When 3D is unavailable or the visitor prefers reduced motion, provide the same information in a static presentation.

These are development directions; mobile benchmarks and model-usability checks have not yet been completed.

## 11. First-Version Preparation Sequence

1. **Verify existing assets and build the public inventory:** reuse private asset identifiers and role records, update obsolete flags using the user's latest confirmation, exclude still-restricted material, and record files, sources, roles, collaborators, publication scope, and process steps.
2. **Select representative elements:** choose forms and excerpts suited to a shared narrative and confirm their recognizability and model quality.
3. **Prototype one scene sequence:** validate the dark hero, large positioning text, and one scroll transition; check reading and interaction on desktop and mobile.
4. **Add one explanatory interaction:** choose form inspection or process steps based on the assets, with source/role information.
5. **Complete the single page:** add other assets, light method sections, professional summary, bilingual content, and contact details.
6. **Prepare publication quality:** check model cost, keyboard and touch operation, reduced-motion fallbacks, public asset contents, and deployment results.
