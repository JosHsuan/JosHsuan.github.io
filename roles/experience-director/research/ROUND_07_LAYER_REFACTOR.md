## New interdisciplinary decision

Interactive Experience Direction, the assigned Software Architect and Performance Engineer exchanged the following critique during this round:

- Software Architect identified the current Canvas `z-index` promotion and per-frame semantic rectangle collection as an unnecessary coupling between DOM readability and GPU output. Direction agrees to replace that architecture, rather than adjust the color of its cutouts.
- Direction proposed stable rear rendering with explicit rounded DOM material planes. Cross-layer influence remains visible through synchronized light/edge color, clear source windows, framing and the existing motion response.
- Performance Engineer supports removal of semantic masking, but notes that removing early shader exits can increase fragment work in formerly hidden pixels. Mask removal alone is therefore not a demonstrated optimization; target allocation, optical cost and sustained behavior must be measured together.
- All three roles reject mandatory large or numerous live backdrop blurs. Direction accepts a glass-like visual impression made with tint, a fixed readability floor, a thin highlight and restrained shadow. Actual blur is an optional measured enhancement, never the means by which text becomes legible.

These are design and engineering decisions, not evidence that the crash is fixed. Root owns comparable baseline/refactored profiling, integration and publication.

Its bounded backing can follow the existing opening-copy footprint with a quiet rim. Model-study headings and captions need their own readable backing; the central `[data-model-viewport]` must not acquire an opaque card fill.

## Material and cross-layer interaction

1. Keep the graphite/off-white/orange palette and IBM Plex. Use a nearly opaque neutral graphite core for reading. Any tint/gradient variation is confined to a restrained rim or outer material region, not a broad moving black gradient across the page. Actual contrast is checked over the brightest and darkest scene states; a chosen alpha value is not proof of contrast.
2. Preserve existing rounded shadowboxes, nested source-image radii and bounded shadows. A thin light-facing edge and subdued opposite edge express depth. Avoid stacking multiple large shadows, full-panel animated filters or repeated compositor surfaces.
3. Reuse the final chapter light direction/color and bounded energy to tint a panel rim or small accent. Do not compute a second lighting simulation, sample the rendered frame, or add a timer. Publish shared presentation values from the existing controller only when a perceptible quantized value changes; suspend with the existing lifecycle.
4. Pointer/focus response belongs to the currently engaged surface. Existing transform choreography and feedback continue; a local edge highlight can reinforce them without moving the outer hit region. No continuous glow sweep over body text. Coarse input and Reduced retain stable boundaries.
5. Source diagrams retain their opaque paper background and original paths/colors. Photographs retain the approved orange presentation and accessible original reveal. Shared light never recolors their documentary content or changes a figure's factual meaning.
6. Preserve autonomous source silhouettes, local focus, light movement, traveling ASCII and chapter identity in the open canvas areas. Effects can be evaluated at a measured bounded resolution/cadence without changing their authored durations or removing chapter states. If sustained device evidence requires lower cost, keep the same composition and semantic interactions first; remove only optional sampling cost in a documented order.

The owner's Apple-material suggestion is a direction for depth and separation, not a requirement to use a particular CSS primitive. WebKit's [backdrop-filter explanation](https://webkit.org/blog/3632/introducing-backdrop-filters/) describes capture, filtering and compositing of the content behind an element, and warns that extra passes have a performance cost. This is mechanism guidance, not a current Safari benchmark. This round does not claim that CSS tint alone is physically correct optical glass or a reproduction of Apple's proprietary interface material.

## Acceptance required before release

The root's actual Build 3 entrance captures found the study title leaving its fixed backing and a diagram title briefly overlapping its hint. Integration now animates each study header/caption as one text-and-material group; diagram headings and hints also enter together. Native source hit regions and controls remain stationary. The phone opening gains 18 CSS pixels of additional internal side space so its existing heading anticipation stays over the reading surface. These are observed boundary corrections, not a redesign of the chapter camera or timing. Final recapture and browser checks are recorded in the case's Round 07 verification.

Subsequent WebKit isolation found that the full-area opening pseudo-element and its outer shadow were costly even after density reduction. Opening and study-label materials now paint directly on their containers; compensating padding/margins preserve the intended bounds and flow contribution. Phone versions use inset-only shadows, while rounded outlines and thin shared-light rims preserve depth and separation. Completed entrances drop identity 3D/filter transforms; live feedback retains its perspective. The field, source optics and MSAA were retained after diagnostic removal did not explain the phone regression.

- Compare baseline and refactored built artifacts on the same browser/device settings. Record work per frame, target dimensions/counts, allocation stability and sustained behavior rather than inferring improvement from a short green E2E run. Do not label emulation as iPhone hardware evidence.
- Inspect all seven chapters at desktop and portrait sizes, including bright/dark loop beats, intermediate scroll positions, reverse transitions, study interaction and original evidence reveal. No black mask intrusion, missing scene states, unreadable text or opaque model aperture.
- Prove a permanently rear Canvas and the absence of semantic cutout work. Confirm continuous light/camera/ASCII still advance when stationary, direct real-model comparisons remain available and source geometry/artwork/typography are unchanged.
- Confirm native scroll, focus, history, caption/layout stability, Pause, Reduced, hidden-tab suspension and bounded context/failure fallback. Fallback must preserve the article without retries that repeatedly recreate the renderer.
- Repeated visits and a meaningful sustained soak must show bounded resources and no observed crash in the tested environments. The exact iPhone failure is not declared reproduced or cured without physical-device evidence; describe that verification limit while shipping the measured resilience and cost corrections.
- Root completes categorized commits, exact-head CI, push, Pages deployment and live checks under the owner's existing authorization. This direction document itself is not a completed release claim.

## Integrated review outcome

Root completed 26 Build 6 Chromium captures across phone, desktop and 4K, including all seven chapters, source apertures and entrance samples. Opened views confirm distinct reading boundaries, retained source visibility and the corrected grouped captions. No capture reports horizontal overflow, page error, extra Canvas or foreground promotion. Slow-device density intentionally softens source edges; it does not change source geometry. Partially entering phone content can still pass behind fixed navigation and remains reachable by native scrolling.

The final matched profiles improve desktop Full traversal from 2.37 to 11.45 FPS and phone-viewport WebKit stationary reading from 12.39 to 16.04 FPS. WebKit traversal improvements are modest; physical Safari crash resolution remains unverified. All 60 browser scenarios have passing results.
