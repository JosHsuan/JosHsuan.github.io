## Reading benefit and actual-pixel acceptance

The intended measurable benefit is complete-source separation from the introductory glass rail: no crop of the embedded simulation label, no prose laid across the still, and a readable original caption and full-image entry at native reading positions. Desktop can use the existing adjacent composition; mobile must remain image-first native flow, without pinning, added clocks or forced simultaneous visibility of all content.

At approximately 342 CSS pixels wide, the 1920-pixel original is substantially reduced. Its embedded labels and fine pale lines cannot be promised to be individually legible inline; high-DPR capture does not restore source detail at that reading size. The native caption must carry the simulation distinction and the full-image action must remain available. No contrast manipulation, cropping or invented physical-photo semantics should be used to hide this limit. If actual mobile pixels show an effectively blank or unrecognizable image with no useful source-reading benefit, do not force the adaptation merely for route uniformity.

Acceptance requires actual C/mobile opening, caption and summary images, followed by the preserved model/three-film/native-Credits/reverse journey, static and failure/source-access policies, fresh history and the required three-profile/full qualification. Film advancement, same-owner continuity and performance must come from appropriate behavioral evidence, not still images. Native image-reading and lifecycle cases in separate browser contexts cannot establish a shared lease across those contexts. No implementation acceptance, actual visual GO, performance improvement or publication is claimed here; this document records the approved scope and original-source reading only.
